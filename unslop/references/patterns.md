# Diagnostic patterns

Use this catalog for long documents, hard rewrites, or review reports. A pattern is a
reason to inspect a passage, not an automatic edit. Judge it against the source's voice,
purpose, and house style.

## Content without evidence

- **Puffery.** Claims of importance, beauty, innovation, or inevitability replace the
  fact that would justify them.
- **Promotional description.** Adjectives such as *vibrant*, *renowned*, or
  *groundbreaking* tell the reader how to react before showing why.
- **Vague attribution.** *Experts say*, *research suggests*, or *critics argue* appears
  without a source the reader can inspect.
- **Decorative name-dropping.** Organizations or publications are listed without the
  specific claim each one supports.
- **Generic conclusion.** The ending predicts a bright future, restates the thesis, or
  gestures at further exploration without adding a decision or consequence.

Preferred repair: name the sourced fact, actor, action, or result. If the source does not
contain it, narrow or remove the claim instead of fabricating support.

## Prefabricated reasoning

- **Canned contrast.** *Not just X, but Y* or *it is not about X; it is about Y* supplies
  drama where a direct statement would be clearer.
- **False range.** *From X to Y* joins examples that do not form a meaningful scale.
- **Formulaic resilience.** *Despite challenges, X continues to thrive* hides both the
  challenge and the evidence of success.
- **Forced trio.** Three examples, benefits, or adjectives appear because three sounds
  complete, not because the subject has three parts.
- **Artificial balance.** A paragraph invents symmetric pros and cons even when the
  evidence points clearly in one direction.
- **Repeated preview and recap.** The text announces a point, makes it, then summarizes
  it again without a new implication.

Preferred repair: state the conclusion once, then spend the remaining space on evidence,
conditions, or consequences.

## Generic diction

- **Inflated verb.** *Utilize*, *facilitate*, *leverage*, *serve as*, or *stand as*
  replaces *use*, *help*, *is*, or the real action.
- **Abstract technical metaphor.** Words such as *landscape*, *substrate*, *vector*,
  *north star*, or *flywheel* stand in for a concrete system, method, goal, or cycle.
- **Nominalization.** *The implementation of validation* hides the actor and action that
  *the parser validates* exposes.
- **Synonym cycling.** One thing receives several names to avoid repetition, forcing the
  reader to decide whether the names mean the same thing.
- **Modifier stack.** Adjectives and adverbs intensify a weak claim instead of providing
  a measurement or mechanism.
- **Stock AI vocabulary.** Clusters of words such as *delve*, *pivotal*, *intricate*,
  *showcase*, *underscore*, or *tapestry* often signal generic prose. Any one of them can
  still be the right word.

Preferred repair: use the same concrete noun for the same thing and the shortest verb
that states the real action.

## Mechanical sentences

- **Superficial participle tail.** A sentence ends with *highlighting*, *ensuring*,
  *reflecting*, or *showcasing* without naming a separate causal fact.
- **Uniform cadence.** Several sentences share the same length and construction, making
  the paragraph sound generated even when each sentence is grammatical.
- **Clause pile-up.** Qualifications accumulate until the reader must backtrack to find
  the subject and verb.
- **Unclear reference.** *This*, *it*, *they*, or *which* can point to more than one noun
  or to an entire preceding paragraph.
- **Passive fog.** Passive voice hides an actor who matters. Passive voice is fine when
  the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- **Hedge pile.** Several uncertainty markers appear where one calibrated marker would
  preserve the source's confidence.

Preferred repair: make the relationship between clauses explicit, restore the actor when
it matters, and vary rhythm in service of emphasis rather than randomness.

## Mechanical structure

- **Heading inflation.** Every short thought receives a heading, so the hierarchy stops
  conveying importance.
- **Inline-label repetition.** A bold label repeats the first words of the sentence that
  follows it.
- **List reflex.** Prose becomes bullets even when the items are not parallel or scannable.
- **Symmetric blocks.** Every section has the same number and shape of paragraphs despite
  unequal subject matter.
- **Formatting as emphasis.** Bold, title case, emoji, or callouts attempt to create
  importance that the sentence has not earned.

Preferred repair: let structure reveal real hierarchy. Keep lists for parallel items and
headings for genuine sections. Formatting can remain when it belongs to the publication's
voice.

## Chat residue

- **Sycophantic opening.** *Great question*, *you are absolutely right*, or enthusiastic
  agreement delays the answer and rewards the user rather than addressing the work.
- **Service preface.** *Of course*, *certainly*, or *I would be happy to* announces
  willingness instead of doing the task.
- **Fake discovery.** *We found the smoking gun* dramatizes ordinary analysis.
- **Generic sign-off.** *I hope this helps* or *let me know if you need anything else*
  appears without a useful next decision.
- **Cutoff disclaimer.** The text apologizes for missing information instead of naming
  the exact evidence gap and its effect on the conclusion.

Preferred repair: lead with the answer. Name uncertainty where it changes the answer and
offer a next step only when a real next step exists.

## Recover the writer

After removing residue, check that the draft still has a person in it:

- Keep a real opinion when the format permits one.
- Preserve jokes, idioms, dialect, code-switching, and deliberate roughness.
- Keep meaningful repetition used for emphasis.
- Let sentence length follow the thought. Short sentences land; longer ones carry a
  condition, consequence, or turn.
- Use first person when the source owns a judgment or experience.
- Prefer an honest gap to a polished invention.

The finish line is not perfect smoothness. It is clear, specific prose that still belongs
to its writer.
