The article, as read

The new rules of context engineering for Claude 5 models

Thariq · Claude Code @ Anthropic · 2026-07-24

“We removed over 80% of Claude Code's system prompt for models like Claude Opus 5 and Claude Fable 5 with no measurable loss on our coding evaluations.” The premise: guardrails written for weaker models now actively harm, because the model must reason about conflicting signals before it can act.

The six shifts

  • Give Claude rules → let Claude use judgement. “Write code that reads like the surrounding code.”
  • Give Claude examples → design interfaces. Examples “constrain them to a certain exploration space.”
  • Put it all upfront → use progressive disclosure. “Consider having a tree of files that can be loaded at the right time.”
  • Repeat yourself → simple tool descriptions. One instruction, one home.
  • Memory in CLAUDE.md → auto-memory. Claude now saves what's relevant on its own.
  • Simple specs → rich references. Code, test suites, HTML artifacts, rubrics — not prose about them.

On each surface

  • CLAUDE.md: “briefly describe what your repo is for… spend most of the tokens on gotchas. Avoid stating ‘the obvious’.”
  • Skills: “lightweight guides… Avoid making them overconstrained, except in highly important areas.”
  • References: “prefer files that are in code… a HTML mockup will generally produce better results than a description.”
  • Anthropic shipped /doctor to rightsize skills and CLAUDE.md automatically.

The diagnosis — before any individual fix

Your problem is not over-constraint. It's duplication that has drifted.

  • The numbers. The seven Ripe skills total 28,155 words across 32 files. Of that, 7,375 words sit in the seven SKILL.md routers — the layer that loads eagerly the moment a skill triggers. Every one of them is 2–3× the ~500-word budget the new guidance implies. A realistic target is ~2,160 words, a 70% cut to the always-loaded layer with no content loss — it moves to on-demand files.
  • The doctrine is orphaned. the-ripe-method.md is 5,958 words, 14 parts, 13 diagrams — and a grep across all seven skill directories returns zero references to it. Nothing can load it partially because nothing loads it at all. Meanwhile the skills re-derive its content from memory, which is exactly how the nine contradictions happened.
  • Repetition is the root cause, not a symptom. “Components don't fetch; listeners hydrate” is stated nine times across six files. Tests live in __tests__/ is stated six times across four skills. The no-transient-props rule appears five times. Each restatement is a fork that can drift — and four of them already have. Applying shift #4 (single source of truth) first is what makes shifts #1 and #3 safe to apply at all.
  • Your own instincts were already right. TESTS.md is the strongest artifact in the repo against the new guidance — verbatim trigger prompts, a scoring rubric, pass thresholds, and a failure→structural-fix lookup table. Scenario 6 deliberately probes whether a rule is defended, not just documented. That is the article's thesis, written by you a year early. Don't rewrite it; re-baseline it.
  • Don't hand-edit all of this. Anthropic shipped /doctor specifically to rightsize skills and CLAUDE.md. Run it first on each skill and let it do Phase 2–3 mechanically; reserve your attention for Phase 0 and Phase 1, which are judgement calls a tool cannot make.

Sequencing — why this order

Roughly 19 hours, and the first two hours matter most

  • Phase 0 before everything. Five defects are producing wrong code right now. They're also small — about two hours total — and none of them depend on any restructuring decision.
  • Phase 1 before Phase 2. You cannot safely delete a duplicate until you've decided which copy is right. Resolving the nine contradictions first turns Phase 2 from a judgement exercise into a mechanical one.
  • Phase 3 last among the content phases, deliberately. Softening rules while nine of them still exist in triplicate would just produce nine softened copies. Deduplicate, then soften the survivor.
  • A realistic shape: Phase 0 + 1 in one evening (~5h, and it's the highest-value block by a wide margin). Phases 2 and 4 are the bulk (~9h) and are good candidates for parallel subagents, one per skill. Phases 3, 5 and 6 are ~5h of cleanup and measurement.
  • What not to do: don't rewrite TESTS.md, don't touch the override clause in CLAUDE.md, and don't cut the examples listed as survivors in P4-1. Those are the parts already doing what the article asks.
